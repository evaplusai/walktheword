The rooms are beautiful. The hallways are missing.

Design 01 gets the hard things right — the paper voice, the grade grammar, the receipt-before-trust card, honest silence. What it never designs is the experience between the screens: no way to move among the eight surfaces, no way back once you walk, no moment where the product teaches its own vocabulary. This page keeps the language and adds the hallways — nine findings, and a working wireframe you can walk yourself, below.

Keep every pixel of the voice. Fix wayfinding, memory of the walk, and the first five minutes.

    Findings ledger — ranked by harm to a first-time reader

    F1Blocker No navigation exists between the eight surfaces. From the reading view there is no route to the Doorway, Notebook, or any Person/Thread/Event screen — each Design 01 wireframe is an island. Fix: the shelf + the Books sheet — Proposals 01·A and 01·B
    F2Blocker Walking has no way back. “Walk to Isaiah 6 →” is the product’s core verb, yet nothing records the step or offers a return. Three walks deep, the reader is lost — the exact anxiety a walkable text must never create. Fix: the trail — Proposal 02
    F3Major The grade vocabulary is never taught. A/B/C is the product’s central idea, but its legend lives only in this design document. A first-time reader sees colored dots and guesses. Fix: three doors on first open — Proposal 03
    F4Major Grade is encoded by color alone. Green vs. blue vs. amber underlines with a 7px tick — indistinguishable for red-green color-blind readers (~8% of men) and invisible at reading speed. Fix: shape carries the grade too — Proposal 03
    F5Major Two text boxes, one confused user. “Moment doorway” and “Ask the archive” are both empty inputs with different rules. Nobody will learn which is which; they’ll type the same words into both. Fix: one Search box, three honest answers — Proposal 04
    F6Major Event columns don’t fit a phone. 150px columns force 12px serif — below comfortable reading size — and the 55%-opacity “peek” column reads as disabled, not swipeable. Fix: one witness at full width — Proposal 05
    F7Major No way to jump to a reference or another chapter. A pastor who needs John 3:16 has no path to it; even Matthew 14 is unreachable from Matthew 13. Fix: the Books sheet (Proposal 01·B) reaches any chapter in two taps; Search accepts references (“mt 14”, “john 3 16”) as a first-class input.
    F8Minor Touch targets are below minimum. 16px pins, superscript verse numbers, and tightly-set dotted phrases sit under the ~44px comfortable tap size. Fix: whole-phrase tap zones with generous line-height (1.85), pins only in design docs, never in product.
    F9Minor The edition footer spends a permanent row on rare needs. Accountability matters; a persistent strip doesn’t. Fix: fold “Ed 1” into the shelf as its quietest item — one tap opens the full plate (changelog, methodology, corrections, verify).

    Unchanged — the parts Design 01 already gets right

    The paper voice. Book serif for scripture, mono for receipts, no brand color competing with the text. Nothing here touches it.
    The connection card. Grade → verbatim target → warrant link → one verb. The best screen in the set; Proposal 03 only adds a “what does A mean?” tap on the chip.
    Honest silence. “The text does not name its author” as a first-class answer stays exactly as drawn.
    The constitution. Every proposal below was checked against §7: the shelf is not engagement chrome, the trail is on-device and self-clearing, nothing composes content.

    Proposal 01·A · fixes F1, F9 · annotated still — live above

    The shelf — four plain words on every screen

    WayfindingNo jargon in the chrome

    A quiet bottom bar: Books (go anywhere in the Bible — Proposal 01·B), Trail (the steps you’ve walked, and the way back), Search (type anything — a feeling, a question, a reference), Notes (your private notebook). The labels are deliberately ordinary words: a tab a reader has to ask about is a failed tab. The product’s poetic vocabulary — walking, doors, doorways — lives in the copy, never in the chrome.

    Three doors — the grade system taught in one screen

    First-run onlyReplayable from any chip

    One screen before first reading: the three grades in plain words, each shown as it will actually appear. From then on, tapping any grade chip anywhere replays its one-line meaning. And shape now carries the grade alongside color — A is solid, B is dotted, C is dashed — legible to every eye and at reading speed.

    Before building — the five-tester verdict, PRD §11’s own standard

    Three hallway tasks that decide whether these fixes worked

    1. The lost-reader test. “Start in Matthew 13, follow any two connections, then get back to where you started.” Pass: returns without help, under 15 seconds, no wrong taps
    2. The vocabulary test. After two minutes of free reading: “what’s the difference between this underline and that one?” Pass: explains A vs C in their own words
    3. The search test. “Find something for a friend whose father just died — then find out who wrote Hebrews — then open John chapter 3.” Same input box. Pass: never asks “which search do I use?”
